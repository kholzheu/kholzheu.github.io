Worker Wealth and the Option of Self-Employment

Authors: Kerstin Holzheu

Status: Working paper

Manuscript version: September 2026

Abstract

Self-employment is widespread in Brazil, where informal businesses rarely borrow. We use Brazilian panel data to estimate a labor-market model in which workers save out of wages to finance business entry, and wages respond to their self-employment options. The estimates imply that two-thirds of labor-market entrants cannot finance entry, while wage adjustment retains employees who can. Targeted grants expand self-employment and raise welfare. Better savings opportunities raise welfare while reducing self-employment, as financial assets become more attractive than business ownership. Relaxing firms’ collateral constraints has little effect. Financial development need not promote entrepreneurship to improve workers’ welfare.
